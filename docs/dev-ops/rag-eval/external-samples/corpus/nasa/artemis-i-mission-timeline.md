## Mission Overview

Artemis I was an uncrewed integrated flight test of NASA deep space exploration systems. The flight combined the Orion spacecraft, the Space Launch System rocket, and ground systems at Kennedy Space Center.

Mission facts:

- Launch date: Nov. 16, 2022.
- Splashdown: Dec. 11, 2022.
- Mission duration: 25 days, 10 hours, 53 minutes.
- Total distance traveled: 1.4 million miles.
- Re-entry speed: 24,581 mph (Mach 32).

The mission demonstrated Orion in a spaceflight environment and prepared the program for later crewed Artemis missions. This sample does not contain the crew roster for Artemis II.

## Flight Day 1

Artemis I launched from Kennedy Space Center on Nov. 16, 2022. Early mission events included launch, max dynamic pressure, solid rocket booster separation, service module fairing jettison, launch abort system jettison, core stage main engine cutoff, Orion solar array wing deployment, perigee raise maneuver, trans-lunar injection burn, and separation from the interim cryogenic propulsion stage.

## Return Phase Overview

The return phase included multiple trajectory correction burns, a final close Moon approach, preparation of recovery assets, spacecraft system checks, and entry operations. A correct long-context answer should connect the return powered flyby, recovery preparation, final trajectory correction burns, service module separation, entry interface, and splashdown.

## Flight Day 20

On Flight Day 20, Orion performed return trajectory correction burn-3 and the returned powered flyby burn. The spacecraft made its second and final close approach to the Moon before the returned powered flyby burn. That burn committed Orion to a Dec. 11 splashdown. Mission managers also polled go to deploy recovery assets off the coast of California.

Expected details for deep retrieval:

- Return trajectory correction burn-3.
- Returned powered flyby burn.
- Second and final close approach to the Moon.
- Recovery assets prepared for Dec. 11 splashdown.

## Flight Day 21

On Flight Day 21, Orion performed return trajectory correction burn-4. The spacecraft exited the lunar sphere of gravitational influence for the last time during the mission.

## Flight Day 24

On Flight Day 24, teams in Mission Control Houston conducted spacecraft system checks ahead of planned splashdown. Recovery teams moved toward the landing area off the Baja Coast near Guadalupe Island. Flight controllers activated the crew module reaction control system heater and tested thrusters as planned.

## Flight Day 25

On Flight Day 25, Orion was on its last full day in space. Return trajectory correction burn-5 was planned. Recovery forces had arrived near the Baja Coast landing area. Engineers also conducted the final Artemis I in-space developmental flight test objective related to solar array wing temperature effects from plume exhaust.

## Flight Day 26

Flight Day 26 contained the final entry and recovery sequence. Key events were:

- Return trajectory correction burn-6.
- Crew module/service module separation.
- Crew module raise burn.
- Targeted entry interface.
- Crew module entry interface.
- Splashdown.

NASA reported the Orion spacecraft completed a parachute-assisted splashdown in the Pacific Ocean as the final major milestone of Artemis I. Orion was recovered by the USS Portland off the coast of Baja California.
