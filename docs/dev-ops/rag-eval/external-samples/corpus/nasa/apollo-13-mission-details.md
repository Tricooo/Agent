## Mission Objective

Apollo 13 was intended to be the third lunar landing attempt. The crew was supposed to land in the Fra Mauro area of the Moon. The mission did not land because an explosion on board forced Apollo 13 to circle the Moon and return to Earth. The Fra Mauro landing site was later reassigned to Apollo 14.

The core mission outcome is therefore mixed: the landing objective failed, but the crew survived and returned safely after a major spacecraft emergency.

## Crew

The Apollo 13 crew included James Lovell, Fred Haise, and Jack Swigert. Days before the mission, backup lunar module pilot Charles Duke inadvertently exposed the crew to German measles. That exposure affected crew assignment decisions before launch.

This sample does not include long-term medical follow-up results for each astronaut after the mission.

## Mission Highlights

At about five and a half minutes after liftoff, the crew felt vibration. Then the center engine of the S-II stage shut down two minutes early. The remaining four engines burned 34 seconds longer than planned, and the S-IVB third stage burned nine seconds longer to put Apollo 13 into orbit.

Later in the mission, a serious problem occurred in the service module oxygen system. The oxygen tank rupture forced the planned Moon landing to be abandoned. The crew and ground teams shifted from exploration to survival and return.

## Emergency Return

Apollo 13 is often described as a successful rescue within a failed landing mission. The reason is that the crew could not complete the Fra Mauro landing objective, but the mission team used the available spacecraft systems and procedures to bring the crew home. A good answer should mention both sides: the lunar landing was aborted, and the crew returned safely.

The lunar module became critical as a lifeboat-like support system after the service module problem. Ground controllers and the crew had to manage limited consumables, trajectory decisions, and spacecraft configuration under pressure.

## Landing And Recovery

Apollo 13 returned to Earth after circling the Moon. The key retrieval point is not a normal lunar landing; the important outcome is safe Earth return after an aborted landing attempt.

## Boundary Notes

This sample supports questions about the mission objective, Fra Mauro, the oxygen-tank emergency, early launch engine behavior, and the broad rescue narrative. It does not support detailed claims about later astronaut health, full telemetry tables, or every engineering procedure used during the emergency.
