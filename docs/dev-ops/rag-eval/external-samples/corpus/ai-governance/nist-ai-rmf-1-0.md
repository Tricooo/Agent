## Framework Goal

NIST AI Risk Management Framework 1.0, often shortened to AI RMF 1.0, is intended to help organizations that design, develop, deploy, or use AI systems manage AI risks. It aims to promote trustworthy and responsible AI development and use.

The framework is intended to be:

- voluntary.
- rights-preserving.
- non-sector specific.
- use-case agnostic.

These properties mean the framework is designed to be flexible across organizations, sectors, and AI use cases.

## Intended Users

The framework can be used by organizations of different sizes and capacities. It is relevant to people and teams involved in AI design, development, deployment, operation, evaluation, and governance.

An answer should not treat AI RMF as a mandatory regulation unless another legal source says so.

## Overview And Development

NIST developed AI RMF through a public and collaborative process. The framework was released on January 26, 2023. NIST describes it as a resource for better managing risks to individuals, organizations, and society associated with AI.

## Companion Resources

The NIST overview page lists companion and related resources that help organizations operationalize the AI RMF. These include:

- AI RMF Playbook.
- AI RMF Roadmap.
- AI RMF Crosswalk.
- Trustworthy and Responsible AI Resource Center, also called the AI Resource Center.
- Generative AI Profile.

The Generative AI Profile is a profile related to risks from generative AI systems. A correct answer should distinguish the base AI RMF 1.0 from later profiles and companion resources.
